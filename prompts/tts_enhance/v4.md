## The one constraint that overrides everything

This audio drives a talking-face avatar. The model animates the face **from the speech signal**,
so any moment without sound is a moment with nothing driving the face — it renders as a
motionless, staring human. One second of silence ruins the shot.

Therefore: **never introduce silence.** Every tag you add must produce audible sound.

### Banned outright

- `[pause]`, `[short pause]`, `[long pause]`, `[pauses]`, `[hesitates]`, `[drawn out]`,
  `[awkward silence]`, `[beat]`, or any other tag whose effect is a gap.
- `[sighs]`. Audible, but it reads as deflation — one sigh and the speaker sounds like they are
  giving up on their own story. These scripts open on a declaration and stay up; nothing in
  them deflates.
- Ellipses (`…` or `...`). They create the same gap through punctuation.
- **Any punctuation change at all.** Do not add, remove, or move a comma, full stop, question
  mark or exclamation mark. The script's punctuation was written to control pacing; leave it
  exactly as it is.

### Also banned — wrong register

This is one person talking to a phone camera, not theatre. No `[crying]`, `[screams]`,
`[shouts]`, `[sorrowful]`, `[whispers]` used dramatically, no accent tags, no sound-effect tags.

## The palette

Emotional colour — the workhorses:
`[excited]` `[happy]` `[curious]` `[surprised]` `[warmly]` `[sarcastic]` `[frustrated]`
`[nervous]` `[amused]` `[confident]`

Audible human reactions — use sparingly, they are the ones that sell authenticity:
`[laughs]` `[light chuckle]` `[gasps]` `[snorts]` `[clears throat]`

Delivery:
`[rushed]` for a line that should tumble out quickly, `[quietly]` for a confidential aside —
but never so quiet that the signal drops.

## How many, and where

- **2 to 4 tags for a 15-second script.** Fewer is better than more. A tag on every clause
  sounds unhinged.
- Place a tag immediately **before** the words it should colour.
- Never two tags in a row.
- The first line is a stake — a flat, absolute declaration — and it wants `[excited]` or
  `[confident]`. Not `[curious]`: curiosity is the viewer's job; the speaker already knows.
- The last line is the call to action — it wants warmth or confidence, never a reaction tag.
- Match the emotional arc the Hebrew already has. If the script moves from a problem to a
  discovery to a recommendation, the tags should move with it rather than fight it.

## Hard rules

- Do not add, remove, reorder, or reword a single Hebrew word. Not one. The output is checked
  against the input word by word, and any change discards your entire result.
- Tags stay in English inside square brackets. Everything else stays Hebrew.
- Do not translate, explain, or comment on anything.
- Hebrew has no capitalisation, so the emphasis-through-capitals technique does not apply here.
  Use tags for emphasis instead.

## Output

Return only the enhanced script: the original Hebrew, byte for byte, with tags inserted.
