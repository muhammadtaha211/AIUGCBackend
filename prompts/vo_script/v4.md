advantages, or the description.

## The arc — guidance, not a rigid template

1. **The struggle.** Specific and relatable, drawn from the problem `what_it_does` solves.
   Open INSIDE the story — the burned-on hook already did the interrupt. No self-introduction
   by name, no meta ("בסרטון הזה…", "אני רוצה לספר לכם…"), no greeting.
2. **What she tried that didn't work** (optional). Generic categories only — NEVER name a real
   competitor, brand, or business.
3. **The discovery.** How she found it. Name the product here, the way a person names something
   to a friend — not the way an ad announces a brand.
4. **What actually changed.** Grounded: only the confirmed advantages, made concrete.
5. **The recommendation.** Addressed directly to viewers with the same struggle. Write this
   beat to work face-to-camera: when the narration outlasts the footage, the persona appears
   on screen and delivers it looking at the viewer.

## Speaker and audience gender — two independent axes

The speaker is {{persona_gender}}. Every script has TWO independent gender axes:
(1) the speaker's own first-person forms, (2) the audience's second-person forms.
Never let one bleed into the other — a male audience does not make the speaker male.

- Every first-person present-tense verb and every adjective the speaker applies to
  herself/himself agrees with speaker gender: ממליצה / מחפשת / בטוחה (female),
  ממליץ / מחפש / בטוח (male). First-person past tense is naturally neutral.
- Audience-addressed forms derive from `target_audience` and use **the same form in every
  single sentence**:
  - One woman → `את`, `שלך`, `תבדקי`, `מפספסת`
  - One man → `אתה`, `שלך`, `תבדוק`, `מפספס`
  - Women → `אתן`, `שלכן`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסות`
  - Men or mixed → `אתם`, `שלכם`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסים`

  Prefer the **singular** — more intimate, more typical of real UGC. Mixing forms is the
  single most obvious machine-tell in Hebrew: never `אתם` in one clause and `לכן` in the next.

Rules:
