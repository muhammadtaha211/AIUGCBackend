You are editing a short Hebrew video ad. An AI presenter speaks to camera about a product; at a few
moments the ad cuts away from the presenter to a stretch of the product owner's own screen
recording of the product, while the presenter's voice keeps going. Your job: for each cutaway,
choose the screen of the recording to show while those words are spoken.

The whole voiceover, with each cutaway's words marked [1: ...], [2: ...] and so on:

{{script}}

The cutaways ({{slot_count}} of them). `words` is what is heard while the cutaway is on screen;
`seconds` is how long it lasts:

{{slots}}

The screens of the recording you may choose from, in recording order. `index` is the screen's id;
`screen` is its name as the analysis read it, `action` what the user is doing on it, `note` why it
might matter, `task` the part of the product it belongs to, and `text` a few lines of text visible on
it. The fields may be in English or Hebrew; match on meaning, not on shared words.

{{states}}

Return `{ "picks": [{ "slot": 1, "state": <index> }, ...] }`, one entry per cutaway, in order:

- Pick the screen that SHOWS what that cutaway's words talk about: the feature named, the result
  promised, the step described. The viewer should feel the product is doing what the voice says.
- The cutaway plays the recording from the chosen screen onward for `seconds`, so prefer a screen
  where something happens next over the very end of a task.
- Every cutaway gets a different screen, and their stretches of the recording must not overlap:
  keep picks at least `seconds` apart in the recording when you can.
- When the words are general (a greeting, a promise, a call to action) and no screen fits better,
  pick a screen that shows the product at its most recognisable — its main screen or its result.
- Use `"state": null` only when no screen relates to the product at all.
- Copy indexes verbatim from the screens' `index` fields; never invent one.
