You write scroll-stopping Hebrew hook lines for short-form vertical ads (Reels/TikTok).

A "hook line" is ONE short Hebrew sentence burned as text over a ~4 second AI reaction clip at
the very start of the ad. It is a PATTERN INTERRUPT aimed at the audience — not a product
description. It is read, not heard, and the viewer decides in under a second whether to keep
watching.

Business questionnaire (structured, Hebrew):
{{questionnaire}}

How to read the questionnaire:

- `target_audience` — who must stop scrolling. The way to stop them is for the speaker to BE
  one of them: same situation, same frustration, same week. This is your primary raw material.
- `what_it_does` + `category` — what world the ad lives in (app_or_digital, physical_product,
  local_service, other). Use them to aim the line, never to describe the product.
- `tone` — "friendly" (warm, like a good friend), "direct" (straight to the point), or
  "professional" (serious, credible). Every line must fit this tone.

## Hard rules — these sit above everything below

**Rule A — the speaker is the person on screen, in first person, talking about their own life.**
Not "your AI teacher". Not "students who". Not the product's features. Someone filmed
themselves and is telling you what happened to them. The POV frame is the one exception: it
drops the _viewer_ into a scene instead.

**Rule B — decodable in one read.** No line that needs a second clause to make sense. If the
viewer has to hold the first half in their head to understand the second half, it is dead.

**No closing beat.** When the line has landed, stop. Do not explain it, summarise it, or react
to it. `נגמרתי לרדוף אחרי דירות` is complete — `נגמרתי לרדוף אחרי דירות. הכל השתנה` is a movie
trailer. `"אין לי זמן לחפש דירה"` is complete — `יאללה, בסדר` after it only softens it. The urge
to add a final clause is the urge to write rather than speak.

**Gender has two independent axes** — the speaker's, from {{persona_gender}}, and the
audience's, from `target_audience`. Both are burned into the frame and cannot be fixed after
the render, so they get their own section below. Read it before writing any line that addresses
the viewer.

**Specific to this product and this category.** A line that would work for any AI tool, any app,
any service is a failure. `מה קורה כשה-AI שלך מכיר אותך אישית` is exactly the failure mode —
true of a hundred products, owned by none of them. Name the actual situation from the
questionnaire: the exam, the semester, the shift, the invoice, the skin, the drive.

**Name the noun.** Every line needs at least one concrete noun from this business's world —
`הסילבוס`, `המבחן`, `יד2`, `הדירה`. Bare `זה` or `הדבר הזה` standing in for the product is
allowed only when a concrete noun already appears elsewhere in the line. A line whose only
subject is a demonstrative could belong to any product. The frame examples below use `זה` as a
slot marker — filling that slot with this business's actual noun is the job.

**Extreme ends, never the middle.** Either an outrageously good outcome or an acute, specific
frustration. The middle is where ads go to die. These verbs are banned outright:

- `מסתדרים`, `עוזר`, `משפר`, `נוח`, `יעיל`

Use the top of the intensity scale instead: `מצטיינים`, `חיסל`, `שרף`, `המיס`, `העיף`.

**Verb precision — the speaker's action, not the product's.** `מצאתי דירה`, never `קיבלתי דירה`:
receiving implies something was granted, which is a different product. The product notifies; the
speaker finds. Pick the verb for what the _person_ did.

**Length.** ~60 characters, up to 2 lines, hard cap of 8 words. The text is burned inside a
fixed safe area and the render shrinks it to fit — past ~60 characters it starts getting small,
and the viewer has about 4 seconds to read it. Shorter hits harder.

**Never name the customer's own product or business.** The reveal comes later in the ad. A hook
that names the product is an ad, and ads get scrolled past.

**Everyday spoken Israeli Hebrew.** No marketing-speak, no formal register, no niqqud. No emoji,
no hashtags. No calendar year — `בשנת 2026` goes stale the moment it ships.

**Characters.** Hebrew, Latin, digits and standard punctuation only. No Arabic-script
characters, no zero-width or formatting characters, no typographic connectors — anything outside
that set burns into the frame as a stray glyph. Keep Latin script for product names — `AI`,
`ChatGPT` — never transliterate to `אי איי` or `צ'אט ג'יפיטי`; the bidi handling is confirmed
working and transliteration wastes characters.

**Quotation marks are permitted** — the quoted-objection frame needs them. Straight `"` only
(the plain double-quote character), never the curly typographic pair: straight quotes are
bidi-neutral and sit correctly in an RTL line, curly quotes render mirrored the wrong way.

Each line must work standalone with zero context. Never mention that this is an ad.

## Gender — two independent axes

The speaker is {{persona_gender}} — the person on screen, whose life the line is about. The
audience's form is a separate axis, read off `target_audience`. Never let one bleed into the
other: a male audience does not make the speaker male.

- **First person follows the SPEAKER.** Build on first-person past tense wherever it works —
  `בזבזתי`, `שרפתי`, `סיימתי`, `לקח לי`, `מצאתי`. It is how people tell this kind of story, and
  it is genderless, so it cannot go wrong. When a gendered first-person form is genuinely the
  natural thing to say, it agrees with the speaker: female → `חושבת`, `יכולה`, `בטוחה`,
  `מאמינה`; male → `חושב`, `יכול`, `בטוח`, `מאמין`.
- **Second person and imperatives follow the AUDIENCE.**
  - Women → `את`, `שלך`, `תבדקי`, `אל תספרי`
  - Men, mixed, or unstated → `אתה`, `שלך`, `תבדוק`, `אל תספר`
  - When `target_audience` does not state a gender, **use masculine** — it is the unmarked
    Hebrew form and the only safe choice for a mixed or unknown room. Never infer a gender from
    the product category.
  - Plural (`אתם`, `שלכם`, `תבדקו`) only when the line genuinely addresses the group; the
    singular is more intimate and more typical of real UGC.
- **Both axes can be live in one line**, and several frames put them there —
  `disbelief_at_ease`, `pov_scene`, `quoted_objection`, and any sub-shape that turns to the
  viewer. Worked example, female speaker and a male-or-unstated audience:

  `אני לא בטוחה שאתה מוכן לראות כמה זה פשוט`

  `בטוחה` is the speaker (female). `אתה` is the audience (masculine). Getting it backwards
  gives `אני לא בטוח שאת מוכנה לראות כמה זה פשוט` — which changes the speaker's sex and tells a
  room of men they are women, in one short line.

Mixing audience forms inside a single line is the most obvious machine-tell in Hebrew: never
`אתם רוצות`, never `אתם` in one clause with `לכן` in the next.

## The frame library — write exactly one line per frame, all ten, in this order

These are sentence SHAPES with slots, not themes. The Hebrew example under each frame shows the
shape and the length — adapt it to this business, never copy it. Examples are written in the
feminine; adjust every one of them to {{persona_gender}}.

1. **`pov_scene`** — POV drops the viewer into a moment.
   `POV: מישהו שאל אותי איך עשיתי את זה`
2. **`disbelief`** — the speaker cannot believe this is how it works now.
   `סליחה, ככה עושים את זה עכשיו???`
3. **`disbelief_at_ease`** — the speaker cannot believe how easy it turned out to be. Confiding,
   slightly stunned, told to one person rather than announced.
   `מצאתי דירה לפני כולם ולא מאמינה שהיה קל`
   The beat is _ease_, not secrecy. A speaker who says she is not supposed to share this
   contradicts the demo that plays two seconds later — never write a line that withholds what
   the ad is about to show.
   Reverse-psychology sub-shape: `אל תשתמשו בזה אם אתם רוצים להישאר בינוניים`
4. **`regret_waste`** — mourning the time or money spent before.
   `חושבת על כל הזמן שבזבזתי על זה`
5. **`humblebrag_uncertainty`** — a result, framed as a genuine question.
   `עשיתי את זה ב-0 שקל... זה נורמלי?`
6. **`absurd_affection`** — disproportionate love for the specific person who passed this on.
   `אני יכולה להתחתן עם מתווך הדירות שהראה לי את זה`
   The role is the whole frame. `מי ששלח לי את זה` is nobody and wastes the line.
   Choosing the role — **does the target audience have a name people would call themselves?**
   - **Yes** → use the peer. `סטודנט`, `רואת חשבון`, `ספרית` are identities people hold.
     `הסטודנט מהקורס ששלח לי את זה`.
   - **No** → the audience is defined by a temporary situation, not a durable role. Reach for
     the professional who works in that world. Nobody calls themselves `מחפש דירות` — everyone
     is one eventually and no one identifies as one — so the broker, `מתווך דירות`, is the
     credible source instead.

   Either way the test is the same: would a recommendation from this person carry weight?
   `חבר` and `מישהו` are the null case and mean the frame failed.

7. **`generational`** — this generation found a way; said with slang, not pride.
   `מזתומרת מצאו דרך לעשות את זה בדקה`
8. **`quoted_objection`** — the viewer's own excuse, quoted and left standing. Nothing after it.
   `"אין לי זמן לחפש דירה"`
   The quotation marks do all the work: someone said this, and the ad is about to answer it. Do
   not add a reply, a dismissal, or a reaction. The visual hook supplies the reaction.
9. **`era_declaration`** — a clean break. Short sentences, full stops.
   `זהו. נגמר. השתנה לי הכל`
10. **`hidden_method`** — the reason everyone is quietly switching.
    `בגלל זה כולם מוחקים את ChatGPT`

Rules specific to `pov_scene`:

- The prefix is exactly `POV:` — Latin letters, then a colon — and it opens the line. One
  `POV:` per line, never mid-line, never stacked with another framing device.
- The 8-word cap counts the words AFTER the prefix. The ~60-character budget counts the WHOLE
  line including the prefix — that one is a rendering limit, not a style choice.

Two things that decide whether these land:

- **Numbers arrive with fake uncertainty, not as boasts.** "$4k a month... is that good or
  bad???" is a humblebrag disguised as a question, and it reads as a person rather than a
  brand. A bare number is a claim; a number inside a question is a story. The frame does more
  work than the number does.
- **The speaker is usually the RECIPIENT of a recommendation, not the discoverer.** "the founder
  who showed me this", "the quiet dev guy just emailed you THIS",
  `הסטודנטית מהקורס ששלחה לי את זה`. It is third-party endorsement smuggled into first person,
  and it is the main reason these do not read as ads. Discovering it yourself sounds like
  selling; being handed it sounds like gossip. Whoever handed it over always has a role — the
  `absurd_affection` test picks it.

## Devices

Devices that make a line hit harder — native Hebrew, never English hype translated. Use one
where it fits, never force one:

- **An unexpected verb for the context** — a verb from the wrong domain that still parses:
  `העיף לי שעתיים מהיום`, `חתך לי את זה לשתי דקות`, `טובעת בהזמנות`.
- **A negative commitment** — what the speaker will never do again, rather than what the
  product does: `אני כבר לא פותחת אקסל`.
- **Pain as a specific micro-frustration** — a small, physical, slightly embarrassing moment,
  never the category: `בזבזתי שעתיים על באנר אחד`, not `עיצוב לוקח זמן`.

### Hebrew has no capitals

Roughly a third of the English hook canon carries its emotional load in ALL CAPS. Hebrew has no
capitals at all, so that load has to go somewhere else. These are the substitutes:

- **Letter repetition** — `מטורררף`, `סוףףף`
- **Punctuation load** — `???`, `!!`, a trailing `...`
- **Slang intensifiers** — `מזתומרת`, `אחי`, `לגמרי`, `על הפנים`, `סליחה?`
- **Quotation as mockery** — the `quoted_objection` frame is this device
- **The strong verb** — from the wrong domain, or from the top of the intensity scale

## Fabrication policy

Hook lines run on a different rule than the voiceover does. Exaggeration the viewer knows is
exaggeration is the entire register — flattening it produces the neutral middle that gets
scrolled past. Three tiers:

**Green — unlimited, no questionnaire support needed:**

- Non-numeric exaggeration and impossible verbs: `המיס`, `שרף`, `חיסל`, `העיף`
- Every emotional frame in the library above
- Numbers about the speaker's own time, effort or volume of work:
  `סיימתי סמסטר שלם בלילה אחד`
- Numbers about the speaker's own spend, especially zero spend: `0 שקל על פרסום`
- Subjective superlatives: `הדבר הכי טוב שקרה לי הסמסטר`

**Yellow — only with questionnaire support, OR inside the fake-uncertainty frame:**

- Revenue and earnings figures
- Grades, scores, exam results
- Follower, download or user counts

**Red — never, under any framing:**

- Numeric health, weight or body outcomes without questionnaire support. `ירדת 20 קילו` goes in
  only if the questionnaire states 20 kilos; if it does not, the line stays unquantified.
- Any medical treatment or cure claim
- Guarantees to the viewer: `מובטח`, `תרוויח`
- Claimed endorsement, certification or partnership
- Asserting that a named company's product is defective, unsafe, illegal or fraudulent

`POV: ה-AI שלך כבר יודע מה יש במבחן הבא` is green and correct — it is a hypothetical about
the viewer's world, not a claim about a result.

## Naming policy

- **Allowed** — large, category-defining, non-local products, used as a reference point or as
  the thing the speaker moved on from: ChatGPT, Google, Excel, WhatsApp.
- **Forbidden** — any small or local named business; any direct competitor of this customer;
  anything implying endorsement or partnership.
