## What a caption is

A caption tells the viewer what to DO, or what the product DID FOR THEM. It never names a UI
element and it never reads a label off the screen.

Test every caption before you return it: would someone who has never seen this app understand
it? If it only means something while looking at that screen, it is a transcription — rewrite it.

Real captions from a shipped plan for a study-materials app, and why each one failed:

- `boteacher שבוע יום היום` — WRONG. Toolbar items read off the screen. Means nothing.
- `הקורסים שלי של boteacher` — WRONG. A navigation item read off the sidebar. The viewer
  learns nothing; they can already see the words.
- `זוהה סילבוס לאן לנתב` — WRONG. "Syllabus detected" describes the file the customer
  uploaded, not the product; "where to route?" is a UI question, not a benefit.
- `חומר קורס אישר ניתוב` — WRONG. A field label welded to a button label. Never glue two
  on-screen labels into one caption — that is transcription with a space in it.
- `שפוך פה הכל` — RIGHT. The product's own tagline, and it tells you what to do.
- `ניתוב הושלם` — RIGHT. This is the target quality: two words, and the product just did
  something for the viewer.

The difference between the last two and the rest is not where the words came from — all of
them are on the screen. It is what the caption is ABOUT. `ניתוב הושלם` is an outcome: the
product finished something for the viewer. `חומר קורס` is furniture: it is the name of a box
on the screen. Read your caption back and ask which of those two you wrote.

## How to build the plan — in this order

**1. Find the ending first.** Scan the timeline for the state where the product finishes the
job: a completed action, a confirmation, a generated output. That state is your LAST step and
the video ends there — no summary step, no call to action, no closing line. An error message,
warning or validation failure is NEVER a success, even when the timeline typed it `result`.

**2. Throw away everything after it.** Real recordings keep rolling after the job is done, and
they often start a whole SECOND journey — a different feature, a different screen, a different
task. That second journey is not part of this ad. Never select a state whose timestamp is later
than your ending, however interesting it looks. Every other step comes from BEFORE the ending.

If the timeline offers no genuine success state at all, do not force one: select the best
journey the recording does show, and explain in `notes` (Hebrew) that the recording has no
clear finish and that the customer should refilm, filming through to a completed action so the
ad can end on it.

**3. Pick the beats before it.** Working back from the ending, choose the states that carry the
journey, in the recording's own order — never reordered. One journey, not a highlight reel: a
contiguous flow a real user would follow, not disconnected impressive screens.

### ONE CAPTION PER VISUAL BEAT — how to decide

Two adjacent candidate states are ONE beat, not two, whenever the viewer would see one screen
with something changing on it rather than two screens. Apply this test, in order, to every
adjacent pair you are considering:

1. Count the `visibleText` entries the two states SHARE, then divide by the length of the
   LONGER of the two arrays. **A third or more of the longer array in common: one screen.**
   Two entries that differ by a letter or two are the same entry — the OCR re-reads the same
   pixels differently frame to frame, so `אישר ניתוב (1)` and `אישור ניתוב (1)` count as
   shared. Divide by the LONGER array, never the shorter one: a dialog that opens on top of a
   page is a new screen even when nearly every word on the small dialog also appears on the
   page behind it, and dividing by the short list would merge the two.
2. Ask what changed. A field that got filled in, a value that got chosen, a button that
   appeared or became enabled, one label swapped for another — all of these are changes WITHIN
   a beat, not new beats.
3. If it is one beat, select the LATER state — the one showing the outcome of the change — and
   drop the earlier one. Let that single clip run longer.

`sameStateAsPrevious` is not this test. That flag marks frames that are literally identical;
two states can both be `false` and still be the same beat under the test above. Trust the test,
not the flag.

opened on top of the dashboard, and the dashboard is a beat of its own. Both get a caption.

### The rest of the selection rules

- Prefer states several seconds apart. Neighbours cut each other short (see the timing rules),
  so a run of near-identical states costs you both length and clarity.
- If the plan comes out too short, the missing length is a beat you SKIPPED somewhere else in
  the journey — a dashboard, a list, a screen the user moved through on the way. It is never a
  second or third caption on a screen you have already used. Two captions on one dialog do not
  buy you time; they cut each other short and read as one confusing moment.
- Never select a state with `legibility: "unreadable"`. Avoid `"low"` unless nothing else
  shows that part of the journey.
- Skip loading screens, splashes, consent banners, empty states, and states marked
  `sameStateAsPrevious`.
- Skip everything that is not the customer using the product itself: login screens, OAuth
  flows and Google/Apple account choosers; onboarding and profile-completion forms; error
  messages, warnings and validation failures; and the app's own in-app product tour (tooltip
  walkthroughs with step counters like 3/11) — the tour is the app advertising itself, not a
  user accomplishing something.
- If a state has nothing meaningful to say, SKIP IT. Never pad: fewer real steps beats an
  inflated count.

**4. Open with the way in.** If a URL appears in any early state's `visibleText`, make the
FIRST step a "go here" instruction naming it — for a state whose `visibleText` contains
`https://example.com`, the caption is `כנסו לexample.com`. Both words are legal: the verb comes
off the instruction list below, the domain off the screen. The home page is often
`legibility: "low"` — take it anyway for this one step; the URL only has to be in the
timeline's text. If no URL appears anywhere in the timeline, skip this step — never invent one.

## How long the ad runs

Each step's clip starts at its own state's timestamp and **is cut off by the next state YOU
selected**. Not the next state in the timeline — the next one in your plan. This is the single
most important number in the whole task, because it works against you twice:

- Two states picked close together produce two SHORT clips, not two full ones.
- A caption that needs longer than its clip is **on screen for less time than it takes to
  read**, and that fails the plan mechanically, exactly like an invented word.

So before you select a state, look at the gap to the state you intend to select AFTER it. That
gap is the ceiling on its clip, and therefore the ceiling on its caption.

Every number in the block below is COMPUTED from the same code that checks your plan — the
read-time function and the step-duration config — so it cannot disagree with the check. The
seconds a caption needs are BOTH the clip it has to be given AND the minimum gap to your next
selected state.

{{timing}}

A step with no neighbour after it — your ending — is never capped this way.

A caption is never allowed below the shortest row of that table, so a gap that only fits the
shortest row forces the shortest caption you can write and still say something. If the only
honest caption for that state is longer, that is the timeline telling you the two states are
one beat.

The totals table gives what each shape runs. Caption length does not move the total — a step's
clip runs its floor length whether its caption is three words or six — so the only levers on
length are HOW MANY beats you select and how far apart they sit. A shape whose total sits under
the band needs one more genuinely different beat from elsewhere in the journey; one whose total
sits over the band needs the least essential beat dropped.

What you must NOT do is manufacture an extra beat by writing a second caption about a screen
you already used. Two captions on one dialog do not buy you time; they cut each other short,
read as one confusing moment, and are the single most common way this plan comes back wrong. A
short journey told in five full sentences beats the same journey chopped into seven labels.

## Caption rules

- Hebrew, and exactly one of the caption lengths in the timing table above — the longest row is
  the hard ceiling, subject to the gap ceiling. Aim at the middle rows: a caption of one or two
  bare nouns lifted off the screen (`חומר קורס`, `יעד ניתוב`) is a field label, not a caption —
  it tells the viewer nothing they could not see. The shortest row is only enough when those
  words are a complete statement on their own (`ניתוב הושלם`, `שפוך פה הכל`).
- Voice: someone narrating their own screen to a friend sitting next to them. Not onboarding
  copy, not marketing. No exclamation marks. No "!וזהו, סיימת".
- Every content word must appear in that state's `visibleText`, in the questionnaire, or in
  the customer's demo description above. This is checked mechanically after you answer — a
  word that appears in none of those places fails the whole plan. Take the product's own nouns
  off the screen, then arrange them into a statement a stranger would understand.
- Two exceptions to that rule, and only two:
  - **Instruction verbs.** These plural imperatives are always allowed, on any step, whether or
    not they appear on screen:
    `כנסו` `היכנסו` `הכנסו` `לחצו` `בחרו` `העלו` `פתחו` `הקלידו` `כתבו` `גררו` `סמנו` `מלאו`
    `שלחו` `חפשו` `שמרו` `גללו` `אשרו` `אישרו` `התחילו` `התחלו`
    Use these exact forms and no others. Two ways this goes wrong:

    - The present-tense forms you would naturally reach for — נכנסים, ממלאים, מעלים, לוחצים —
      are NOT on the list and will fail the check.
    - A verb that is merely *similar* to one on the list is not on the list. `הזינו` is not
      there. `הורידו` is not there. `זיהה` is not there. The check compares whole words, so
      "close enough" fails exactly like a word you invented.

    If the verb you want is not on the list, do NOT fall back to copying the button's label as
    your whole caption — that is how `חומר קורס אישר ניתוב` happened. Instead say what the
    screen achieved, using its nouns: `אשרו את הניתוב` rather than `אישר ניתוב`.
  - **Function words and pronouns** (של, את, על, זה, שלכם, שלו, אותו, במקום, …) are always
    allowed.
- Product-benefit words — what the product does automatically, instantly, for free — must come
  from the QUESTIONNAIRE or the customer's demo description, and in the questionnaire
  specifically from its `what_it_does` and `advantages` fields. Read those before you write,
  and use their words: they are the customer's own claims and they are legal on every step. If
  the customer never said it, do not write it. A claim the recording does not show and the
  customer never made is not yours to invent.

## Before you answer

Go back over every caption one word at a time. For each word, find it in **that state's own**
`visibleText` — not a neighbouring state's, however similar the two screens look — or in the
questionnaire, or in the customer's demo description, or on the instruction-verb list, or
among the function words. If a word is in none of those five places, replace it now.

The near miss is what fails, every time. The check compares whole words, so a word that is
almost right fails exactly like one you invented:

- the screen reads `זוהה` and you want to write `זיהה` — use `זוהה`
- the screen reads `ניתוב` and you want to write `לנתב` — use `ניתוב`

The OCR is imperfect and it clips and mis-spells words. Its spelling still wins: copy character
for character from where you found the word, even when the result is slightly odd Hebrew.

But copying the spelling of a word is not the same as copying a label as your caption. If the
only way you can make a caption legal is to read a box's name back to the viewer, you have
picked the wrong state — go back to step 3, merge it into the beat next to it, and caption the
outcome instead.

Then check the arithmetic, all four:

1. Does the plan total inside the band named in the timing block?
2. Does it end on the success state, with every other step before it?
3. For each step, is the gap to your NEXT selected state at least as long as its caption needs?
4. Is any pair of selected states the same beat under the test in step 3?
