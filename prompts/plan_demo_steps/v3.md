## What a caption is

A caption tells the viewer what to DO, or what the product DID FOR THEM. It never names a UI
element and it never reads a label off the screen.

Test every caption before you return it: would someone who has never seen this app understand
it? If it only means something while looking at that screen, it is a transcription — rewrite it.

Real captions from a shipped plan for a study-materials app, and why each one failed:

- `boteacher שבוע יום היום` — WRONG. Toolbar items read off the screen. Means nothing.
- `זוהה סילבוס לאן לנתב` — WRONG. "Syllabus detected" describes the file the customer
  uploaded, not the product; "where to route?" is a UI question, not a benefit.
- `חומר קורס אישר ניתוב` — WRONG. A field label welded to a button label. Never glue two
  on-screen labels into one caption — that is transcription with a space in it.
- `שפוך פה הכל` — RIGHT. The product's own tagline, and it tells you what to do.
- `ניתוב הושלם` — RIGHT. This is the target quality: two words, and the product just did
  something for the viewer.

## Selection rules

- Chronological, never reordered. The video plays the recording's own order.
- One journey, not a highlight reel — a contiguous flow a real user would follow, not
  disconnected impressive screens.
- ONE CAPTION PER VISUAL BEAT. When two adjacent candidate states show the same screen with
  only a small change — a field filled in, a button that became enabled, one label swapped for
  another — that is ONE beat, not two. Select the state that shows the OUTCOME and let its clip
  run longer. A single caption the viewer can finish reading beats two that flash past.
- OPENING STEP. If a URL is legibly visible in the early states, open the ad with a "go here"
  instruction naming it (`כנסו ל...`). If no URL is clearly visible anywhere in the timeline,
  skip this — never invent one.
- The LAST step must be the result/success state. The video ends there: no summary step, no
  call to action, no closing line. A genuine success is a completed action, confirmation or
  generated output — an error message, warning or validation failure is NEVER a success, even
  when the timeline typed it `result`. If the timeline offers no genuine success state, do not
  force one: select the best journey the recording does show, and explain in `notes` (Hebrew)
  that the recording has no clear finish and that the customer should refilm, filming through
  to a completed action so the ad can end on it.
- Never select a state with `legibility: "unreadable"`. Avoid `"low"` unless nothing else
  shows that part of the journey.
- Skip loading screens, splashes, consent banners, empty states, and states marked
  `sameStateAsPrevious`.
- Skip everything that is not the customer using the product itself: login screens, OAuth
  flows and Google/Apple account choosers; onboarding and profile-completion forms; error
  messages, warnings and validation failures; and the app's own in-app product tour (tooltip
  walkthroughs with step counters like 3/11) — the tour is the app advertising itself, not a
  user accomplishing something.
- If a state has nothing meaningful to say, SKIP IT. Never pad: fewer real steps beats an
  inflated count.

## How long the ad runs

The steps must total **11–16 seconds** of video. This is checked mechanically after you answer
and a plan outside the band fails.

Each step's clip starts at its own state's timestamp and is cut off by the NEXT selected
state, so two states picked close together produce two short clips, not two full ones. Within
that limit a clip runs about 1.6–2.8 seconds depending on the action and how long the caption
takes to read.

In practice: **6 or 7 steps is the comfortable middle**. Five steps only reaches the band if
its captions are 5–6 words each. Prefer well-spaced states — several seconds apart — over
neighbouring ones.

## Caption rules

- Hebrew. **3–6 words, aiming at 3–5.** Six is the ceiling, not the target.
- Voice: someone narrating their own screen to a friend sitting next to them. Not onboarding
  copy, not marketing. No exclamation marks. No "!וזהו, סיימת".
- Every content word must appear in that state's `visibleText` or in the questionnaire. This
  is checked mechanically after you answer — a word that appears in neither place fails the
  whole plan. Build captions by COPYING the product's own words off the screen, then arranging
  them into something a stranger would understand.
- Two exceptions to that rule, and only two:
  - **Instruction verbs.** These plural imperatives are always allowed, on any step, whether or
    not they appear on screen:
    `כנסו` `היכנסו` `הכנסו` `לחצו` `בחרו` `העלו` `פתחו` `הקלידו` `כתבו` `גררו` `סמנו` `מלאו`
    `שלחו` `חפשו` `שמרו` `גללו`
    Use these exact forms. The present-tense forms you would naturally reach for — נכנסים,
    ממלאים, מעלים, לוחצים — are NOT allowed and will fail the check.
  - **Function words and pronouns** (של, את, על, זה, שלכם, אותו, …) are always allowed.
- Product-benefit words — what the product does automatically, instantly, for free — must come
  from the QUESTIONNAIRE. If the questionnaire does not say it, do not write it. A claim the
  recording does not show and the customer never made is not yours to invent.
