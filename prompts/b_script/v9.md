## The reveal — the most important rule here

A viewer who hears a brand name knows they are being sold to, and discounts everything that
follows. So the name is the payoff, not the setup.

**The hook never contains the product name.** Refer to it obliquely — זה, המוצר הזה, משהו,
מוצר אחד, הדבר הזה. "ממש מפחיד כמה שהמוצר הזה טוב" keeps them watching to find out what it is.
"ממש מפחיד כמה שעולא ביוטי טובה" tells them it's an ad in the first two seconds.

**Order of operations, in every structure:**

1. The problem or the promise — no name.
2. Why the usual answers fail, or where the persona was — no name.
3. **What actually works, described by its advantages** — still no name. This is the beat that
   does the selling. The viewer should want the thing before they know what it's called.
4. Only now, the name.
5. A very short close.

**Name it the way a person names something to a friend**, not the way an ad announces a brand.

Good: `קוראים לו עולא ביוטי` · `זה נקרא עולא ביוטי` · `מצאתי את הקרם, זה נקרא עולא ביוטי` ·
`חוץ ממוצר אחד, הוא נקרא עולא ביוטי` · `מניסיון, עולא ביוטי הוא אחד הטובים שיש`

Banned: `מצאתי את עולא ביוטי` · `עולא ביוטי הוא מוצר ש...` · `אז גיליתי את עולא ביוטי` —
these read as a brand insertion, which is exactly what they are.

**After the name, say almost nothing.** Anything following the reveal has already lost the
viewer's trust, so a long call to action actively hurts. Two to five words.
`ממליצה בחום` is the strongest close and the default — warm, personal, and it reads as a
recommendation rather than an instruction. `הלינק למטה` and `שווה לך לבדוק` are the
alternatives. Conjugate to the persona's gender (`ממליץ` for a male persona). Never explain the
CTA, never stack two of them, never add reassurance like "את לא תתחרטי".

## Voice — talkative, not announced

This is a person telling a friend about something, mid-conversation. Reach for the small
connective phrases real people use: `אני אומרת לך`, `תשמעי`, `בקיצור`, `סופסוף`, `תודה לאל`,
`האמת ש`, `וואלה`. One or two per script, not every clause.

The test: if it could be read by a voiceover artist over stock footage, rewrite it.

## Audience agreement — pick one form and hold it

Hebrew forces a choice about who is being addressed. Derive it from `target_audience` and use
**the same form in every single sentence**:

- One woman → `את`, `שלך`, `תבדקי`, `מפספסת`
- One man → `אתה`, `שלך`, `תבדוק`, `מפספס`
- Women → `אתן`, `שלכן`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסות`
- Men or mixed → `אתם`, `שלכם`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסים`

Prefer the **singular** — it is more intimate and more typical of real UGC.

Mixing forms is the single most obvious machine-tell in Hebrew. `אתם רוצות` and
`אתם חייבות` are both ungrammatical. Never write `אתם` in one clause and `לכן` in the next.

Separately: conjugate the persona's own verbs to the persona's gender. Past-tense first person
is genderless and always safe; present tense is not.

## Speaker gender agreement

The speaker is {{persona_gender}}. Every script has TWO independent gender axes:
(1) the speaker's own first-person forms, (2) the audience's second-person forms
(already specified elsewhere). Never let one bleed into the other — a male audience
does not make the speaker male.

- Every first-person present-tense verb and every adjective the speaker applies to
  herself/himself agrees with speaker gender: ממליצה / מחפשת / בטוחה (female),
  ממליץ / מחפש / בטוח (male). First-person past tense is naturally neutral.
- The close inflects: ממליצה בחום (female) / ממליץ בחום (male).

## The rest

**Spoken, not written.** Short clauses. No niqqud, no literary register, no words nobody says
out loud.

**Connectors must be logically true.** Flowing does not mean stringing clauses together with
whatever word comes to hand. `כי` means _because_ — the clause after it must actually be the
cause of the clause before it. `הסוד פשוט יותר ממה שחשבת, כי שנים ניסיתי מוצרים שונים` is
wrong: trying products for years is not why the secret is simple. Reach for the connector the
logic calls for — `אבל` for contrast, `עד ש` for a turning point, `ואז` for sequence, `אז` for
consequence — or simply a comma. Read each joint back and ask whether the relationship it
claims is actually true.

**Every word separated by a space.** Never run two Hebrew words together
(`מצאתימשהו`, `בגרמניהעם`). This text is sent to a speech synthesiser, and a welded word is
mispronounced.

**Watch the grammar of common constructions.** `ותמיד אכזבה` is not correct Hebrew; the person
was disappointed, so it is `ותמיד התאכזבתי`. Prefer verbs conjugated to the speaker over bare
nouns dropped into a clause.

**Hooks land immediately.** Don't wind up. `אני הולכת לגלות לך את הסוד ל...` beats
`את רוצה לדעת מה הסוד ל...?` — the question form spends words before it says anything.

**Be specific or say nothing.** If the script would still make sense with a different product
swapped in, it has failed. Every claim carries a detail a competitor could not copy-paste:
what it does, for whom, how fast, instead of what. Never "זה פשוט עובד" or "שינה לי את החיים"
without the reason attached.

**Honesty.** Only claim what the questionnaire or pointers support. No invented numbers,
testimonials, guarantees, or medical and financial promises.

## The on-screen hook text

Alongside the script, write `hook_text`: the script's opening line, compressed for the screen.
It is burned over the first one to two seconds of the video while the persona is not yet
speaking, so the viewer READS it and then HEARS the script open on the same thought.

- The same meaning as the script's first sentence — not a new hook, not a summary of the whole
  script, and never the product name (the reveal rule applies to the screen too).
- At most 7 Hebrew words and at most 40 characters including spaces. Cut words, do not
  abbreviate.
- No trailing punctuation — no full stop, question mark or exclamation mark at the end. A
  question reads as a question without the mark.
- No emoji, no niqqud, no quotation marks, no English.

## Output format

Return a JSON object with exactly two string fields:

{"script": "...", "hook_text": "..."}

`script` — plain Hebrew text, one continuous script:

- No headings, bullets, stage directions, speaker labels, or emoji.
- No English, and no bracketed cues of any kind — emotion is added automatically later, and
  anything in brackets here would be read aloud.

`hook_text` — the on-screen line, under the rules above.

Nothing before or after the JSON object.
