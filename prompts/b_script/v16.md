## The reveal — the most important rule here

A viewer who hears a brand name knows they are being sold to, and discounts everything that
follows. So the name is the payoff, not the setup.

**The hook never contains the product name.** Name its KIND instead — `הקרם הזה`, `האתר הזה`,
`האפליקציה הזאת`, `הקורס הזה` (`product_kind` above, or the noun you derived from
`what_it_does`). Never `משהו`, never `הדבר הזה`, never `מוצר אחד`: a word that could mean
anything tells the viewer nothing, and it reads as a machine dodging a noun rather than as a
person who has not said the brand yet.
"ממש מפחיד כמה שהקרם הזה טוב" keeps them watching to find out WHICH cream it is — they know
what kind of thing is coming and still not whose it is, which is exactly the tension you want.
"ממש מפחיד כמה שעולא ביוטי טובה" tells them it's an ad in the first two seconds.

**Order of operations, in every structure:**

1. The problem or the promise — no name. Build its first sentence so that its first 4–6 words
   already stand alone as a hook — `hook_text` below is cut verbatim from exactly those words,
   and 7 is the hard ceiling, not the target.
2. Why the usual answers fail, or where the persona was — no name.
3. The name, casually — the way a person names something to a friend, not the way an ad
   announces a brand.
4. The one or two strongest advantages, concrete. This is the beat that does the selling:
   what it does, for whom, how fast, instead of what.
5. The closer — one of the offered closers, and nothing after it.

**Name it the way a person names something to a friend**, not the way an ad announces a brand.

Good: `קוראים לו עולא ביוטי` · `זה נקרא עולא ביוטי` · `מצאתי את הקרם, זה נקרא עולא ביוטי` ·
`חוץ מקרם אחד, הוא נקרא עולא ביוטי` · `מניסיון, עולא ביוטי הוא אחד הטובים שיש`

Banned: `מצאתי את עולא ביוטי` · `עולא ביוטי הוא מוצר ש...` · `אז גיליתי את עולא ביוטי` —
these read as a brand insertion, which is exactly what they are.

**The closer is the last thing said.** Pick ONE of the offered closers and copy its text.
Conjugate ONLY what its line allows:

- A speaker-axis closer follows the persona's gender: `ממליצה` → `ממליץ`, `חוזרת` → `חוזר` for
  a male persona.
- An audience-axis closer follows the form every other sentence in the script uses:
  `תנסו` → `תנסי` for one woman, `תנסה` for one man; `תנסו` stays for a plural audience.
- A closer marked plural-audience-only may be chosen only when the script addresses a plural
  audience. Otherwise choose another of the offered closers.
- Everything else is copied exactly — no synonyms, no added words, no reordering.

Keep its punctuation exactly as offered — it is a tested delivery control, not decoration.

Nothing follows the closer — no call to action, no link, no reassurance like "את לא תתחרטי".
The closer is the final word of the script.

## Voice — talkative, not announced

This is a person telling a friend about something, mid-conversation. Reach for the small
connective phrases real people use: `אני אומרת לך`, `תשמעי`, `בקיצור`, `סופסוף`, `תודה לאל`,
`האמת ש`, `וואלה`. One or two per script, not every clause.

The test: if it could be read by a voiceover artist over stock footage, rewrite it.

## Imperfections and intensity — budgeted, never random

A script with no seams sounds written. Include **one or two** of these, and no more:

- **A filler dropped mid-clause** — `כאילו`, `בקיצור`, `זהו`.
- **An interrupted upgrade** — the speaker outbids their own sentence mid-flight:
  `זה חסך לי, לא, זה מחק לי את כל השלב הזה`.
- **An afterthought** — `אה, ובלי להתקין כלום` — before the closer, never after it.

Rules for them:

- Never in the first five words. The opening has to land, and `hook_text` is cut from it.
- Never in the closing line. The closer is the last thing said, and it is clean.
- Never two in a row.
- They cost words. Under a budget of about 35 words, use exactly one.
- **No written-out vocal sounds** — not `אמ`, not `אהה`, not `המממ`. Every word is aligned and
  burned on screen as a caption, so a non-word shows up as gibberish and the synthesiser's
  reading of it is unpredictable.
- **No ellipses** (`…`). A comma carries the same beat. (The closer's `..` is not one — see
  "Punctuation is delivery" below.)
- **Never `רגע`** as a filler, a restart, or an aside — it must not appear in a script.

Intensity is a separate budget. **At most one intense device per script** — native Hebrew,
never English hype translated:

- **An unexpected verb for the context** — a verb from the wrong domain that still parses:
  `העיף לי שעתיים מהיום`, `בלע לי את כל הרשימות`, `שרפתי על זה שנתיים`.
- **Negative commitment** — what the speaker will never do again, rather than what the product
  does: `אני כבר לא פותחת אקסל`, `לא אכנס לעוד הרצאה בחיים`.
- **Exclusion** — the group being left behind, never a person: `מי שעדיין עושה את זה ידנית, מפסיד`.

One is the budget. Two already reads as trying; three is a caricature.

## Audience agreement — pick one form and hold it

Hebrew forces a choice about who is being addressed. Derive it from `target_audience` and use
**the same form in every single sentence**:

- One woman → `את`, `שלך`, `תבדקי`, `מפספסת`
- One man → `אתה`, `שלך`, `תבדוק`, `מפספס`
- Women → `אתן`, `שלכן`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסות`
- Men or mixed → `אתם`, `שלכם`, `תבדקו`, `מפספסים`

Prefer the **singular** — it is more intimate and more typical of real UGC.

Mixing forms is the single most obvious machine-tell in Hebrew. `אתם רוצות` and
`אתם חייבות` are both ungrammatical. Never write `אתם` in one clause and `לכן` in the next.

Separately: conjugate the persona's own verbs to the persona's gender. Past-tense first person
is genderless and always safe; present tense is not.

## Speaker gender agreement

The speaker is {{persona_gender}}. Every script has TWO independent gender axes:
(1) the speaker's own first-person forms, (2) the audience's second-person forms
(already specified elsewhere). Never let one bleed into the other — a male audience
does not make the speaker male.

- Every first-person present-tense verb and every adjective the speaker applies to
  herself/himself agrees with speaker gender: ממליצה / מחפשת / בטוחה (female),
  ממליץ / מחפש / בטוח (male). First-person past tense is naturally neutral.
- A speaker-axis closer inflects the same way: אני לא חוזרת אחורה (female) /
  אני לא חוזר אחורה (male).

## The rest

**Spoken, not written.** Short clauses. No niqqud, no literary register, no words nobody says
out loud.

**Punctuation is delivery, and the closer's is fixed.** The speech synthesiser reads
punctuation as delivery, and the closers' marks were chosen by ear. The closer keeps exactly
the punctuation it was offered with: `..` there is two full stops by design, not an ellipsis
to be tidied to one dot or three; `!!!!` is deliberate, not a typo to be reduced. Everywhere
else in the script, write ordinary punctuation.

**Connectors must be logically true.** Flowing does not mean stringing clauses together with
whatever word comes to hand. `כי` means _because_ — the clause after it must actually be the
cause of the clause before it. `הסוד פשוט יותר ממה שחשבת, כי שנים ניסיתי מוצרים שונים` is
wrong: trying products for years is not why the secret is simple. Reach for the connector the
logic calls for — `אבל` for contrast, `עד ש` for a turning point, `ואז` for sequence, `אז` for
consequence — or simply a comma. Read each joint back and ask whether the relationship it
claims is actually true.

**Every word separated by a space.** Never run two Hebrew words together
(`מצאתיקרם`, `בגרמניהעם`). This text is sent to a speech synthesiser, and a welded word is
mispronounced.

**Watch the grammar of common constructions.** `ותמיד אכזבה` is not correct Hebrew; the person
was disappointed, so it is `ותמיד התאכזבתי`. Prefer verbs conjugated to the speaker over bare
nouns dropped into a clause.

**Hooks land immediately.** Don't wind up. `אני הולכת לגלות לך את הסוד ל...` beats
`את רוצה לדעת מה הסוד ל...?` — the question form spends words before it says anything.

**Be specific or say nothing.** If the script would still make sense with a different product
swapped in, it has failed. Every claim carries a detail a competitor could not copy-paste:
what it does, for whom, how fast, instead of what. Never "זה פשוט עובד" or "שינה לי את החיים"
without the reason attached.

**Honesty.** Only claim what the questionnaire or pointers support. No invented numbers,
testimonials, guarantees, or medical and financial promises.

Naming other products:

- **Allowed** — large, category-defining, non-local products, used as a reference point or as
  the thing the speaker moved on from: ChatGPT, Google, Excel, WhatsApp.
- **Forbidden** — any small or local named business; any direct competitor of this customer;
  anything implying endorsement or partnership.
- Never assert that a named company's product is defective, unsafe, illegal or fraudulent.

## The on-screen hook text

Alongside the script, write `hook_text`: a VERBATIM PREFIX of the script's own opening line —
the first 4–6 words, character for character, exactly as they appear in `script`.
7 is the hard ceiling, not the target. It is burned over the first one to two seconds of the
video while the persona is not yet speaking, so the viewer READS those exact words and then
HEARS the script open by saying them.

- **Verbatim, not compressed.** Not a paraphrase, not a restatement, not a shorter way of
  saying the same thing — the literal opening substring of `script`, unedited. If no prefix of
  the opening line you drafted both stands alone as a hook and fits the caps below, rewrite
  that opening line until one does — this is a constraint on how the script itself opens, not
  only on how it is summarised.
- Cut at a natural break — the end of a clause, a comma, the point where the opening thought is
  already legible on its own — never mid-word, never leaving a dangling comma or conjunction at
  the end. Cut words, do not abbreviate.
- At most 7 Hebrew words and at most 40 characters including spaces.
- Never the product name (the reveal rule applies to the screen too — and beat 1, where this
  prefix comes from, never carries it anyway).
- No trailing punctuation — no full stop, question mark or exclamation mark at the end. A
  question reads as a question without the mark.
- No emoji, no niqqud, no quotation marks, no English.

## Output format

Return a JSON object with exactly two string fields:

{"script": "...", "hook_text": "..."}

`script` — plain Hebrew text, one continuous script:

- No headings, bullets, stage directions, speaker labels, or emoji.
- No English — except a product name allowed by the naming policy, written the way the brand
  writes it (ChatGPT, Excel).
- No bracketed cues of any kind — anything in brackets is read aloud.

`hook_text` — the on-screen line, under the rules above.

Nothing before or after the JSON object.
